# Report and presentation outline

Adapt this outline to the lecturer's actual format and length requirements. Bracketed prompts require the group's own work; they are not completed results.

## 1. Title and project identity

Use the agreed title, class group and official group number. State that this is a retrospective analysis of public, sequence-derived marker calls from archived samples.

## 2. Biological background and rationale

Explain malaria drug resistance, the role of PfCRT and the meaning of K76T using primary literature. Describe related Kilifi studies. Explain why sensitivity to mixed calls and archive composition matters for this particular question. Avoid claiming that no one has studied the trend before.

## 3. Research question, scope and objectives

Use the agreed wording from the proposal, revised after feedback. Define the population actually available for analysis, the 1994–2020 range and the sample-level outcomes. Do not describe the archive as a random survey of all Kilifi residents.

## 4. Data and methods

- Cite Pf8 and give the exact bundled source files and retrieval date.
- Explain the sample identifier join, location/year filters, source QC and recorded case-group check.
- Show the inclusion counts and marker classification mapping.
- Define both denominators mathematically and explain mixed and missing calls.
- State the predefined periods, Wilson intervals and sensitivity analyses.
- Explain any exploratory test, its assumptions and what it cannot establish.
- Record software versions and identify the reproducible notebook/script.

## 5. Results

Start with data coverage and exclusions. Present category counts, annual/period summaries, uncertainty and sensitivity findings. Cite each table or figure in the text. Report denominators with proportions; do not copy only a p-value. Separate observations from explanations.

Recommended figures: annual sample counts; marker composition; primary versus secondary prevalence with confidence intervals. Include a compact sensitivity table if it changes interpretation. Do not add charts simply to increase the figure count.

Caption template: “[What is shown] in [which samples and years]. [Outcome definition and denominator]. [Interval method, if used]. [Meaning of missing years]. Source: MalariaGEN Pf8; group analysis.”

## 6. Discussion and limitations

Compare findings with Omedo et al. and Wamae et al. Check whether samples or source studies may overlap before calling those comparisons independent validation. Discuss mixed infections, sampling intensity, contributing studies, assay/callability constraints, recorded versus unrecorded repeats and the single-marker scope. Explain why population-level selection is a possible interpretation rather than an experimentally established cause in this analysis.

The archive ends in 2020. State that these data do not determine current prevalence, direct drug susceptibility or clinical treatment policy. A K-only call at this codon does not establish the sequence or phenotype of the entire parasite genome.

## 7. Conclusion

Answer the agreed question using the evidence actually obtained, including whether sensitivity checks materially changed the interpretation. Identify one specific follow-up that addresses a limitation.

## 8. References and supporting material

Use the reading guide and `references.bib` as a starting bibliography. Cite sources you actually read. Attach or link the reproducible notebook, data dictionary, inclusion audit and contribution record as the course permits.

## Presentation practice

Every member should be ready to answer:

1. Why this gene, this codon, this location and this period?
2. How did 2,078 Kilifi metadata records become 1,872 retained samples?
3. What exactly does a mixed K/T call mean for each denominator?
4. Why are 2001 and 2002 blank in prevalence tables?
5. Why do some intervals look wider even when the proportion is similar?
6. What changes when you restrict to the largest study?
7. How does the project differ from published work and classmates' work?
8. Which conclusion is supported, and which tempting conclusion exceeds the data?
